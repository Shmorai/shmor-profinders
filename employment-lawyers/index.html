What can an employment lawyer help with?

  • Settlement and severance agreements
  • Unfair or wrongful dismissal (termination) claims
  • Tribunal, agency and court representation
  • Discrimination and whistleblowing matters
  • Restrictive covenants and contract disputes
  • Business transfers, layoffs and redundancy advice

When should you hire an employment lawyer?

  • You have received a claim, such as an employment tribunal claim (UK) or an EEOC charge (US)
  • You have been offered, or need to offer, a settlement agreement
  • You are dismissing someone and want to minimise legal risk
  • A dispute involves discrimination, whistleblowing or a senior employee

Questions to ask before you hire

When you compare quotes, these questions help you find the right fit:

  1. Are you licensed by the state bar (US) or regulated by the SRA or your national bar or law society (UK/EU)?
  2. How much of your work is employment law specifically?
  3. Do you offer fixed fees for settlement or severance agreements?
  4. Who will handle my case day-to-day?

What affects the cost?

Every business and project is different, so prices vary. The main factors are:

  • Fixed-fee vs. hourly billing
  • Stage of the dispute (advice, negotiation, tribunal)
  • Seniority of the lawyer handling the matter
  • Complexity and value of the claim

The simplest way to understand the cost for your situation is to compare a few quotes side by side.

Employment Lawyers: frequently asked questions

When should I speak to an employment lawyer?
As early as possible — ideally before you take action such as a dismissal, or as soon as you receive a claim. Time limits for employment claims can be short.
Who pays for a settlement or severance agreement review?
In the UK, employers often contribute towards the employee’s legal fees for reviewing a settlement agreement. In the US, employees usually pay for their own review. A lawyer can confirm what applies to you.
Can a lawyer represent my business in an employment dispute?
Yes. Employment lawyers advise businesses and represent them in employment tribunals (UK), labour courts (EU), and agency or court proceedings (US).
