Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: who must join, and what to do next

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD for Income Tax) is HMRC's new way for sole traders and landlords to do Self Assessment, and it started on 6 April 2026 for the largest group. Whether you are in now or due in 2027 or 2028, here is how the thresholds work, which dates matter, and when professional help pays for itself.

What is Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

GOV.UK describes it as a new way for sole traders and landlords to do Self Assessment. You only need it if you are already registered for Self Assessment, have submitted a tax return, and have income from self-employment as a sole trader, property as a landlord, or both.

Some things stay the same: you still submit one tax return a year and pay your bill, both by 31 January after the end of the tax year. What changes is how you get there. You need to use compatible software, keep digital records of your self-employment and property income and expenses, send quarterly updates every three months, and then submit your return through the software.

Who has to join, and when?

The start date depends on your qualifying income: your total turnover from self-employment and property, before expenses, taken from an earlier tax return. HMRC's timetable is:

  • 6 April 2026: qualifying income over £50,000 (based on your 2024 to 2025 return).
  • 6 April 2027: over £30,000 (based on your 2025 to 2026 return).
  • 6 April 2028: over £20,000 (based on your 2026 to 2027 return).

Note it is turnover, not profit. A sole trader with £55,000 of sales and £20,000 of costs still counts as over £50,000. GOV.UK also lists exemptions, so if you think you may qualify for one, check the official guidance or ask an adviser. From September 2026, HMRC said it would begin signing up people who need to use the service for 2026 to 2027 and have not done so themselves, using only the information it already holds, which may be out of date. You can also volunteer early to practise.

Quarterly update deadlines for 2026 to 2027

If your accounting period ends on 5 April, you start digital records from 6 April 2026 (from 1 April if it ends on 31 March). HMRC's first-year timetable gives these deadlines:

  • First quarterly update: 7 August 2026
  • Second: 7 November 2026
  • Third: 7 February 2027
  • Fourth: 7 May 2027
  • Tax return and tax bill: 31 January 2028

Quarterly updates are summaries of income and expense totals, not tax returns, and each one covers the tax year so far. Even if you had no income or expenses in a period, you must still send the update. You do not need to make accounting or tax adjustments before sending one. After each update you can see an estimate of your tax bill for the year to date.

Penalties: what changes

HMRC says new penalties replace the current late filing and late payment ones. If you are late with a quarterly update or your return, you get a penalty point first, and a penalty only follows when you reach a points threshold, so a one-off slip does not cost you straight away. HMRC has said it will not apply penalty points for late quarterly updates for 2026 to 2027, although you still need to send them before you submit your return. Late payment penalties are also being changed so that the sooner you pay, the lower the penalty. Check GOV.UK for the current details, as rules may change.

A practical checklist for getting ready

  1. Check your qualifying income on your most recent relevant tax return, and note which start date applies.
  2. Check your accounting period, as it decides whether you use standard (5 April) or calendar (31 March) update periods.
  3. Choose software that records income and expenses for every business you have, and that can also add other income sources before you submit your return.
  4. Move your records into it, with the amount, date and category of each income and expense.
  5. Put the dates in your calendar, including the 7th of the month after each quarter ends.
  6. Decide who sends the updates: you, a bookkeeper or an accountant. If an agent acts for you, they need to be authorised.

None of this replaces advice on your own circumstances, so confirm the details on GOV.UK or with a qualified adviser.

Software, spreadsheets and getting help

HMRC does not provide the software. You can pick an all-in-one product, or use more than one. Spreadsheets are still allowed, but you need software that links to them (often called bridging software) to send updates. HMRC's software finder lists products that have passed its recognition process.

Many small businesses will find it easiest to ask an accountant or bookkeeper to set this up and send the updates. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax in 2026 to 2027?
Only if your qualifying income, your total turnover from self-employment and property before expenses, was over £50,000 on your 2024 to 2025 tax return. The threshold falls to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028 (GOV.UK).
Is the threshold based on profit?
No. GOV.UK defines qualifying income as total turnover from self-employment and property income before expenses.
Can I still use a spreadsheet?
Yes, but you also need software that links to it, sometimes called bridging software, to send quarterly updates and submit your return.
Do I still need to file a tax return?
Yes. You still submit one tax return a year and pay by 31 January after the tax year ends, but you now do it through your compatible software after sending quarterly updates.
