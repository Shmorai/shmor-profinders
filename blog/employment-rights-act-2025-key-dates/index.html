Employment Rights Act 2025: the dates UK employers need to know

The Employment Rights Act 2025 is being phased in through to 2027. Some changes are already live, others arrive within weeks. Here is the timetable in the government's own latest update, and the practical steps worth taking now.

By the ProFinders editorial team · 19 November 2026 · 2 min read

Where the Employment Rights Act 2025 stands (as of November 2026)

The Act received Royal Assent in December 2025 and is being brought in stage by stage. This article follows the GOV.UK timeline update published on 5 November 2026. Dates have already moved once, so check GOV.UK before acting on any single date. It applies to employers in Great Britain unless a specific measure says otherwise.

Already in force

  • 6 April 2026: day-one paternity leave and unpaid parental leave; the collective redundancy protective award maximum doubled; Statutory Sick Pay with the lower earnings limit and waiting period removed; stronger whistleblowing protection for reports of sexual harassment.
  • 7 April 2026: the Fair Work Agency was established.
  • 1 October 2026: the time limit to bring most Employment Tribunal claims rose from 3 to 6 months. For breach of contract claims in Scotland the change took effect on 9 November 2026.

Coming soon: 30 October 2026 onwards

  • 30 October 2026: employers must take all reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment, and must not permit third-party harassment. Trade union access and recognition reforms also start.
  • 1 December 2026: Seafarer Protection Regulations.
  • 1 January 2027: the qualifying period for unfair dismissal drops to six months for dismissals from that date, and the compensatory award cap is removed. Employers must also tell workers of their right to join a trade union, and tipping law is strengthened.

2027 and beyond

  • March 2027: fire and rehire protections.
  • April 2027: mandatory gender equality and menopause action plans for private and voluntary sector employers, bereavement leave including pregnancy loss, and the organisation-wide collective redundancy threshold.
  • September to December 2027: limits on NDAs in harassment cases, stronger protection for pregnant women and new mothers and flexible working changes (October), and umbrella company regulation (December).
  • Zero hours contracts: no date yet. Guaranteed hours and notice rules will be timed after consultation.

What to do now

  1. Review your harassment policy and training and record the steps you take, as the duty is to take all reasonable steps.
  2. Tidy up probation and performance processes. With a six-month qualifying period from January 2027, how you manage new starters matters more.
  3. Check contracts, handbooks and absence policies against the new leave and sick pay rules.
  4. Get advice on anything contentious. An HR consultant can update policies, and an employment lawyer can advise on dismissals, settlement agreements and tribunal claims. Larger changes may also need a business lawyer. Our guide on how ProFinders works explains how to compare quotes.

This is general information, not legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

When does unfair dismissal protection start after six months?
According to the GOV.UK timeline update of 5 November 2026, the unfair dismissal qualifying period is cut to six months for dismissals from 1 January 2027, and compensatory awards are uncapped.
What is the new sexual harassment duty for employers?
From 30 October 2026, employers must take all reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment and must not permit third-party harassment.
How long do I have to bring an employment tribunal claim now?
The time limit rose from 3 to 6 months from 1 October 2026 for most claims. The date differs for Scottish breach of contract claims (9 November 2026).
